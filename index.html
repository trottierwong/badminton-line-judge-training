Badminton Line Judge Training

Interactive volunteer training • Approximately 5–10 minutes

Welcome, volunteers!

Learn to judge whether a shuttle lands IN or OUT, use clear hand signals, and handle situations where you cannot see the landing.

Key rule: A shuttle touching any part of the relevant boundary line is IN. Judge where the shuttle first contacts the floor, not where it rolls or where feathers extend.

Watch your assigned line and stay impartial. Make the appropriate signal only after the shuttle has landed; do not anticipate the umpire’s decision on other faults. If you genuinely cannot see the landing, do not guess.

Lesson 1 — Line judge signals

These illustrations show the hands and arm positions. Official line judges should follow the referee's briefing and current BWF instructions.

IN

Point to the line with your right hand. Do not call out.

OUT

Call “OUT!” and extend both arms horizontally.

UNSIGHTED

Cover both eyes with both hands. Never guess.

Lesson 2 — Know the boundary

Singles: The long, narrow court. Doubles: The wider court. For doubles service, the back service boundary is the inner doubles long service line; during rallies, the outer back boundary applies. Singles service uses the outer back boundary.

For every call, use the boundary relevant to singles or doubles and to service or rally play. If unsure which line you are assigned, ask the umpire or referee before play begins. Sit looking directly along your assigned line. Look toward the umpire when signaling. The umpire may overrule a clearly incorrect line call under the Laws.
